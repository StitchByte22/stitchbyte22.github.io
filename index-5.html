What I do

Four areas that usually overlap. Most work starts with the first one.

Business systems

Looking at how the business actually runs today – which systems you use, who uses them, and how information moves between them. I map it out so the duplication, gaps and manual hand-offs are easy to see.

Reporting & data

Turning information you already hold into clear reports and dashboards that people actually use. Less time assembling the numbers, more time looking at them.

Integrations

Connect what you already have. Where it’s practical, getting your systems to pass information to each other instead of someone re-typing it.

Automation

Finding the repetitive jobs – the exports, the copy and paste, the spreadsheet rebuilt every Monday – and working out which can be simplified or automated, and which are better left alone.

How I work

Start with the business, not the software.

  1. 01

    Understand

    What needs to happen, who’s involved and why it matters. I start by listening to how things work now and what you need them to do.

  2. 02

    Map

    Lay out the systems, data and processes involved, so everyone can see how things really work – not just how they’re supposed to.

  3. 03

    Improve

    Identify the practical options: a clearer report, a simpler process, a connection between systems, or a task that can be automated.

  4. 04

    Implement

    Where it makes sense, configure, build or help put the solution in place – and make sure your team understands how it works.

Sometimes the map is enough on its own. A clear picture of how your business really works is useful whatever you decide to do next.

Who I help

You don’t always need a new system. Sometimes you need to understand how the systems you already have can work better together.

I work best with businesses that have grown beyond simple processes and spreadsheets, but don’t need – or want – a large, expensive software project.

This might sound familiar

  • The same information gets typed into more than one system
  • Month-end reporting means exporting, pasting and checking
  • Only one person really understands a key spreadsheet
  • Systems were added as you grew, but never quite joined up
  • You’ve been told the answer is a new platform, and you’re not sure it is

Typical businesses

  • Ecommerce businesses
  • Small manufacturers
  • Growing retailers
  • Service businesses
  • Owner-managed businesses that have outgrown their original setup

Example project – a fictional business

Connecting sales, stock and financial information

A made-up wholesaler, used to show the kind of thinking involved. It sells through a CRM and a website, manages stock in a separate system and keeps its accounts in an accounting package. Each system works fine on its own. The trouble is in between.

What mapping it out shows

Where the same order was being entered more than once, which figures were being reconciled by hand each week, and which reports depended on one person’s spreadsheet.

What changes

Not the systems themselves. The improvements come from connecting them in the right places and bringing the information together into one clearer reporting view.

This is an illustrative example rather than a client project. A fully worked case study is on its way.
